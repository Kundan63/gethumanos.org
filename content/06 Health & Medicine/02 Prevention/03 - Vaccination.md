# Vaccination

> *Vaccination is the process of administering a vaccine to stimulate the immune system to develop protection against a specific infectious disease without causing the disease itself. Vaccines expose the immune system to a safe form or component of a pathogen, allowing it to produce antibodies and immune memory that provide rapid protection during future exposure. Vaccination is one of the most effective public health interventions ever developed, preventing millions of deaths each year and reducing the spread of many serious infectious diseases.* :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# What is Vaccination?

**Vaccination** is the process of giving a **vaccine** to prepare the immune system before natural infection occurs.

The goal is simple:

> **Train the immune system without exposing the body to the full dangers of the disease.**

Vaccination is different from **immunization**.

- **Vaccination** = receiving a vaccine.
- **Immunization** = becoming protected against disease, often as a result of vaccination. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Why does Vaccination exist?

Before vaccines, infectious diseases caused widespread illness and death.

Examples include:

- Smallpox
- Polio
- Measles
- Diphtheria
- Tetanus

Natural infection creates immunity but often at a high cost.

Vaccination allows the immune system to develop protection **without first suffering the disease and its complications.** :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

# The Big Question

> **How can the immune system learn to fight a disease before encountering it naturally?**

Vaccination answers this by safely introducing the immune system to disease-specific antigens, enabling long-lasting immune memory.

### 🦠 Live Attenuated

Contain weakened organisms.

Examples:

- Measles
- Mumps
- Rubella (MMR)

---

### 💀 Inactivated

Contain killed organisms.

Examples:

- Inactivated polio vaccine
- Hepatitis A

---

### 🧩 Subunit / Recombinant / Conjugate

Contain only specific components of the pathogen.

Examples:

- Hepatitis B
- HPV

---

### ☣️ Toxoid

Protect against bacterial toxins.

Examples:

- Tetanus
- Diphtheria

---

### 🧬 Viral Vector

Use a harmless virus to deliver genetic instructions.

Examples:

- Some Ebola vaccines

---

### 🧪 mRNA

Deliver messenger RNA that instructs cells to temporarily produce a harmless antigen, triggering an immune response.

Examples:

- Some COVID-19 vaccines

Different technologies achieve the same goal: training immune memory. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

# What Diseases Can Vaccines Prevent?

Vaccines currently protect against more than 30 serious infectious diseases, including:

- Measles
- Polio
- Tetanus
- Diphtheria
- Pertussis
- Hepatitis B
- Influenza
- Human papillomavirus (HPV)
- Rotavirus
- Rabies
- Yellow fever
- Cholera
- Meningococcal disease
- Japanese encephalitis
- RSV
- COVID-19 (where recommended)

The specific vaccines recommended vary by age, country, occupation, travel, and medical condition. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

# Why should humans care?

Vaccination:

- Prevents serious infectious diseases.
- Reduces hospitalizations.
- Prevents many deaths.
- Protects vulnerable people.
- Helps control outbreaks.
- Contributes to longer, healthier lives.

WHO estimates that immunization currently prevents **3.5–5 million deaths every year** worldwide. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}
