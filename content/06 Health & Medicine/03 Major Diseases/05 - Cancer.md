# Cancer

> *Cancer is a group of diseases in which abnormal cells grow uncontrollably, evade the body's normal growth controls, invade surrounding tissues, and may spread (metastasize) to distant organs. Cancer develops through a multistep process involving genetic changes and interactions with physical, chemical, and biological carcinogens. It can affect almost any tissue in the body and is one of the leading causes of death worldwide, causing nearly **10 million deaths in 2024**. Between **30–50% of cancers are preventable** through evidence-based prevention strategies.* :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# What is Cancer?

Cancer is **not one disease**.

It is a collection of **more than 200 diseases** that share one defining feature:

> **Normal cells lose control over growth and begin multiplying uncontrollably.**

Unlike healthy cells, cancer cells:

- Ignore growth signals
- Resist programmed cell death
- Continue dividing
- Invade nearby tissues
- Sometimes spread throughout the body (metastasis)

---

# Why does Cancer occur?

Every day, billions of cells divide.

Normally:

- DNA is copied accurately.
- Damaged cells repair themselves.
- Severely damaged cells die.

Cancer develops when these protective systems gradually fail due to accumulated genetic changes and environmental exposures. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# The Big Question

> **How does one normal cell become a life-threatening disease?**

Cancer develops through years of accumulated DNA damage that allows abnormal cells to escape normal biological control mechanisms.

```text
 └──────────────┐

```

### Carcinoma

Begins in epithelial tissues.

Examples:

- Lung
- Breast
- Colon
- Prostate

Most common cancers.

---

### Sarcoma

Develops from:

- Bone
- Muscle
- Fat
- Connective tissue

---

### Leukemia

Cancer of blood-forming tissues.

Usually involves bone marrow.

---

### Lymphoma

Cancer of the lymphatic system.

---

### Melanoma

Cancer of pigment-producing skin cells.

# Complete Causes

Cancer almost always results from **multiple interacting causes**.

```text
Complete Causes
      │
 ┌────┼─────────────────────────────┐
 ▼    ▼                             ▼
Genetics
Environment
Lifestyle
```

---

## 🧬 Genetic Factors

- Inherited mutations (5–10% of cancers)
- Family history
- DNA repair disorders

Most cancers are **not inherited** but arise from mutations acquired during life. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

## 👴 Aging

Risk increases with age because:

- DNA mutations accumulate
- Repair systems become less efficient
- Cells divide repeatedly over decades

---

## 🚬 Tobacco

Largest preventable cause.

Associated with:

- Lung cancer
- Mouth cancer
- Throat cancer
- Bladder cancer
- Pancreatic cancer

---

## 🍺 Alcohol

Long-term alcohol consumption increases risk of:

- Liver cancer
- Breast cancer
- Colorectal cancer
- Mouth and throat cancers

---

## 🥗 Nutrition

Higher risk is associated with:

- Low fruit and vegetable intake
- Processed meats
- Excess calorie intake leading to obesity

Healthy dietary patterns are associated with lower risk for several cancers. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

## ⚖ Obesity

Associated with increased risk of multiple cancers including:

- Endometrial
- Breast (postmenopausal)
- Colorectal
- Kidney
- Esophageal

---

## 🏃 Physical Inactivity

Insufficient physical activity increases the risk of several cancers.

---

## ☀ Ultraviolet Radiation

Major cause of:

- Melanoma
- Other skin cancers

---

## ☢ Ionizing Radiation

Examples:

- Medical radiation (when excessive or unnecessary)
- Radon
- Nuclear exposure

---

## 🦠 Infections

Some infections contribute to cancer.

Examples:

- HPV → Cervical cancer
- Hepatitis B & C → Liver cancer
- *Helicobacter pylori* → Stomach cancer
- Epstein–Barr virus → Some lymphomas

Cancer-causing infections account for a substantial proportion of cancers in some regions. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

## 🌫 Environmental & Occupational Exposure

Examples:

- Asbestos
- Arsenic
- Air pollution
- Industrial chemicals

# Hallmarks of Cancer

Cancer cells commonly acquire the ability to:

- Sustain uncontrolled growth
- Ignore growth suppression
- Resist cell death
- Divide indefinitely
- Stimulate new blood vessel formation
- Invade surrounding tissues
- Spread to distant organs
- Evade immune destruction

These are known as the **hallmarks of cancer**.

---

# Common Symptoms

Symptoms depend on cancer type but may include:

- Unexplained weight loss
- Persistent fatigue
- Persistent pain
- Lump or swelling
- Persistent cough
- Blood in stool or urine
- Difficulty swallowing
- Changes in bowel habits
- Abnormal bleeding
- Non-healing sores

Many cancers produce **no symptoms in their early stages**.

---

# Diagnosis

Diagnosis may involve:

- Medical history
- Physical examination
- Blood tests
- Imaging (CT, MRI, PET, Ultrasound)
- Endoscopy
- Biopsy (gold standard)
- Molecular testing
- Genetic testing (selected patients)

---

# Treatment

Treatment depends on:

- Cancer type
- Stage
- Molecular characteristics
- Patient health

Common treatments include:

### Surgery

Removal of tumors.

### Radiation Therapy

Uses high-energy radiation.

### Chemotherapy

Drugs that destroy rapidly dividing cells.

### Targeted Therapy

Targets specific molecular abnormalities.

### Immunotherapy

Helps the immune system attack cancer.

### Hormone Therapy

Used for hormone-sensitive cancers.

### Stem Cell Transplantation

Used mainly in blood cancers.

Many cancers are curable when detected early and treated appropriately. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

---

# Lifestyle Management

Evidence supports:

- Healthy body weight
- Regular exercise
- Smoking cessation
- Limiting alcohol
- Healthy diet
- Sun protection
- Vaccination where appropriate
- Participation in recommended screening

---

# Nutrition Support

### Recommended Foods

- Fruits
- Vegetables
- Whole grains
- Legumes
- Beans
- Nuts
- Seeds
- Fish
- Olive oil

### Helpful Nutrients

- Fiber
- Vitamin-rich fruits and vegetables
- Healthy unsaturated fats

### Limit

- Tobacco
- Alcohol
- Processed meats
- Ultra-processed foods
- Excess calories

No food cures cancer, but healthy dietary patterns support overall health and may lower the risk of developing certain cancers. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

# Ayurveda & Traditional Medicine

Traditional Ayurvedic approaches may include:

- Turmeric
- Ashwagandha
- Guduchi
- Amla
- Tulsi
- Yoga
- Meditation

### Current Scientific Evidence

Some Ayurvedic herbs contain biologically active compounds under active research, and some may help with symptom management or general well-being. However, there is **no high-quality evidence** that Ayurvedic therapies alone can cure cancer. They should be considered complementary and discussed with the oncology team because herbs may interact with chemotherapy or other treatments. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

### Safety

- Do not delay proven cancer treatment.
- Some herbal products interact with chemotherapy.
- Product quality varies.

# HumanOS Principle

Cancer is not a single disease but a diverse group of disorders caused by the gradual accumulation of genetic changes that allow cells to escape normal biological control. Its development depends on interactions among inherited susceptibility, aging, environmental exposures, infections, and lifestyle factors. Although not all cancers are preventable, a substantial proportion can be avoided through evidence-based prevention, and many can be cured or effectively controlled when detected early and treated appropriately. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

---

# Key Insight

> **Cancer is the progressive loss of normal cellular regulation. When accumulated genetic damage allows cells to divide uncontrollably, invade surrounding tissues, and spread throughout the body, a normal process of growth becomes disease. Understanding cancer from its molecular origins to prevention and treatment reveals that reducing risk, detecting disease early, and applying evidence-based therapies together provide the strongest path to saving lives.** :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}
