# Cognition

> *Cognition is the collection of mental processes involved in acquiring, processing, storing, and using knowledge. It includes perception, attention, thinking, learning, memory, language, reasoning, problem solving, judgment, and decision making. Cognition enables humans to interpret information, understand the world, solve problems, adapt to changing environments, and guide behavior. Cognitive psychology is the scientific study of these mental processes and how they interact.* :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# What is Cognition?

Cognition is the process of **knowing and thinking**.

It allows humans to:

- Perceive the world
- Pay attention
- Learn
- Remember
- Think
- Solve problems
- Understand language
- Make decisions

Cognition transforms raw sensory information into meaningful knowledge. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

# Why does Cognition exist?

Every second, the brain receives enormous amounts of information.

Without cognition, humans could not:

- Recognize objects
- Understand language
- Learn from experience
- Plan for the future
- Solve problems
- Adapt to change

Cognition evolved because organisms that could process information more effectively had a better chance of surviving and reproducing.

## 👁 Perception

Perception interprets sensory information.

Examples:

- Seeing a face
- Hearing music
- Recognizing objects

Perception transforms sensations into meaningful experiences.

---

## 🎯 Attention

Attention selects important information.

Because the brain cannot process everything simultaneously, attention determines:

- What we notice
- What we ignore
- What enters conscious awareness

---

## 📚 Learning

Learning produces lasting changes in knowledge, skills, or behavior through experience.

Learning builds the knowledge used by later cognitive processes.

---

## 🧠 Memory

Memory stores information over time.

It involves:

- Encoding
- Storage
- Retrieval

Memory allows previous experiences to influence future behavior.

---

## 💬 Language

Language enables:

- Communication
- Abstract thinking
- Knowledge sharing

Language also influences how humans organize and express ideas.

---

## 💡 Thinking

Thinking combines stored knowledge to:

- Generate ideas
- Analyze situations
- Plan actions
- Imagine possibilities

---

## ⚖ Reasoning

Reasoning draws conclusions from available information.

Types include:

- Deductive reasoning
- Inductive reasoning
- Analogical reasoning

---

## 🧩 Problem Solving

Problem solving identifies solutions to challenges by applying knowledge, reasoning, and creativity.

---

## 🎯 Decision Making

Decision making evaluates alternatives and selects actions based on:

- Goals
- Evidence
- Experience
- Emotions
- Expected outcomes

# Mental Models

The brain simplifies reality by creating internal representations called **mental models**.

These help people:

- Predict outcomes
- Understand systems
- Solve unfamiliar problems

Mental models improve efficiency but can also produce errors when they are inaccurate.

---

# Concepts & Schemas

The brain organizes information into:

### Concepts

General categories such as:

- Tree
- Dog
- Justice

### Schemas

Organized networks of related knowledge built through experience.

Schemas help humans quickly interpret familiar situations but may also contribute to cognitive biases and stereotypes. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

# Common Cognitive Biases

The brain often uses mental shortcuts (**heuristics**) to make decisions quickly.

These shortcuts may produce biases such as:

- Confirmation bias
- Availability heuristic
- Anchoring
- Overconfidence
- Framing effect

Biases increase efficiency but sometimes reduce accuracy.

# Misconceptions

### ❌ Cognition is the same as intelligence.

Reality:

Intelligence is one aspect of cognition. Cognition includes many other mental processes such as perception, attention, memory, language, and reasoning.

---

### ❌ Cognition happens consciously.

Reality:

Many cognitive processes occur automatically without conscious awareness. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}
