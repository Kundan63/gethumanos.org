# HumanOS Decision Framework

> **The HumanOS Decision Framework is a universal method for making thoughtful, evidence-based, and responsible decisions.**
>
> It transforms understanding into action by guiding every important decision through the principles of reality, evidence, systems thinking, ethics, and long-term responsibility.

---

# Purpose

Every human life is shaped by decisions.

Some decisions are small.

Others influence years or even generations.

HumanOS exists to improve decision quality.

Because better decisions produce better lives.

Better lives build better societies.

Better societies improve humanity.

---

# The HumanOS View of Decisions

A decision is not merely choosing between options.

A decision is choosing which future to help create.

Every decision changes:

- yourself,
- other people,
- your environment,
- future possibilities.

Therefore every decision deserves thoughtful consideration.

# The Seven Decision Questions

Before making any important decision, ask these questions in order.

---

## 1. What is reality?

Separate observations from assumptions.

Questions:

- What actually happened?
- What facts are known?
- What remains uncertain?

Reality always comes before interpretation.

---

## 2. What evidence supports it?

Evaluate the quality of available information.

Consider:

- Scientific evidence
- Reliable data
- Direct observation
- Relevant experience
- Expert consensus

Avoid making important decisions from weak evidence alone.

---

## 3. What systems are involved?

No decision exists in isolation.

Ask:

- What caused this?
- What depends on it?
- What else will be affected?

Think beyond the immediate problem.

---

## 4. What are the available options?

Generate multiple realistic alternatives.

For every option consider:

- Benefits
- Costs
- Risks
- Trade-offs
- Opportunity cost

The first solution is rarely the only solution.

---

## 5. Which option best aligns with HumanOS Principles?

Evaluate every option against the HumanOS Principles.

Does it:

- respect reality?
- follow evidence?
- improve capability?
- protect health?
- strengthen relationships?
- support long-term thinking?
- contribute positively?

A technically correct decision may still be a poor human decision.

---

## 6. What are the consequences?

Think across multiple time horizons.

```text
Immediate

↓

Short-term

↓

Long-term

↓

Generational
```

Also consider:

- intended consequences,
- unintended consequences,
- reversible consequences,
- irreversible consequences.

Responsible decisions consider both benefits and costs.

---

## 7. What did I learn?

Every decision creates feedback.

After acting, ask:

- What happened?
- What surprised me?
- What assumptions were wrong?
- What should change next time?

Reflection transforms experience into wisdom.

# The Four Filters

Every major decision should pass four filters.

## Reality Filter

Is it true?

---

## Capability Filter

Does it improve long-term capability?

---

## Ethics Filter

Does it respect people, responsibility, and fairness?

---

## Contribution Filter

Does it leave the world better than before?

Only decisions that pass all four filters should move forward.

# Decision Quality

HumanOS measures decision quality by the quality of the reasoning process, not only by the eventual outcome.

A good process can still encounter bad luck.

A poor process can occasionally produce a good outcome.

Improving the process improves the probability of better outcomes over time.

---

# Common Decision Errors

Avoid:

- Acting before understanding.
- Confusing confidence with evidence.
- Ignoring uncertainty.
- Optimizing one outcome while harming the larger system.
- Thinking only about immediate consequences.
- Allowing emotions to replace reasoning.
- Failing to learn from previous decisions.
