# Neurons

> *Neurons are the fundamental information-processing cells of the nervous system. They receive information, integrate it, generate electrical signals, and communicate with other neurons, muscles, and glands. Every thought, memory, movement, emotion, and perception begins with the activity of billions of interconnected neurons.* :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

# What are Neurons?

A **neuron** (nerve cell) is the basic functional unit of the nervous system.

Unlike most cells, neurons are specialized for **communication**.

Their job is to:

- Receive information
- Process information
- Generate electrical signals
- Transmit information to other cells

The human brain contains approximately **86 billion neurons**, connected through trillions of synapses to form an immense communication network. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Without neurons:

- No thoughts
- No memory
- No movement
- No sensation
- No emotions
- No learning
- No consciousness

Everything the brain does ultimately depends on neurons.

---

# Why do Neurons exist?

A multicellular organism requires rapid communication.

Every second the body must:

- Detect light.
- Hear sound.
- Feel touch.
- Move muscles.
- Regulate organs.
- Remember experiences.
- Respond to danger.

Chemical diffusion alone is far too slow.

Evolution therefore produced specialized cells capable of transmitting information rapidly across long distances.

Neurons solve this communication problem.

# Anatomy of a Neuron

```text
        Dendrites
      /\   |   /\
     /  \  |  /  \
        \  | /
         \ | /
      ┌─────────┐
      │  Soma   │
      │Nucleus  │
      └─────────┘
           │
           ▼
      Axon Hillock
           │
══════════ Axon ══════════
     ███      ███
   Myelin   Myelin
      │         │
   Node     Node
      │
      ▼
Axon Terminals
      │
      ▼
 Synapse
```

### 🌳 Dendrites

Receive incoming signals from other neurons.

Think of them as the neuron's **input antennas**.

---

### 🧫 Cell Body (Soma)

Contains:

- Nucleus
- Organelles
- Energy-producing mitochondria

The soma integrates incoming information and maintains the cell.

---

### ⚡ Axon

A long projection that carries electrical impulses away from the cell body.

Some axons extend more than a meter in length.

---

### 🛡 Myelin Sheath

A fatty insulating layer surrounding many axons.

Functions:

- Increases signal speed
- Reduces energy use
- Protects the axon

Loss of myelin slows nerve transmission, as occurs in diseases such as **multiple sclerosis**. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

### 🔹 Nodes of Ranvier

Small gaps between myelin segments.

Electrical signals "jump" between nodes, greatly increasing conduction speed (saltatory conduction). :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

### 🌐 Axon Terminals

The communication points where neurons release neurotransmitters to the next cell.

# Types of Neurons

```text
               Neurons
                   │
     ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
     ▼             ▼             ▼
 Sensory      Interneurons    Motor
 Neurons                       Neurons
```

### 👁 Sensory Neurons

Carry information **from receptors to the brain and spinal cord**.

Examples:

- Vision
- Hearing
- Touch
- Pain
- Temperature

---

### 🧠 Interneurons

Connect neurons within the brain and spinal cord.

Responsible for:

- Thinking
- Learning
- Memory
- Decision-making

Most neurons in the human brain are interneurons.

---

### 💪 Motor Neurons

Carry commands **from the brain and spinal cord to muscles and glands**, producing movement and bodily responses.

# Why should humans care?

Everything you experience depends on neurons.

They enable you to:

- Read
- Speak
- Walk
- Think
- Remember
- Feel pain
- Experience joy
- Solve problems
- Learn new skills
- Build relationships

Damage to neurons can affect:

- Memory
- Movement
- Speech
- Vision
- Personality
- Consciousness

Protecting neurons means protecting your ability to live independently.

| Disorder | Primary Problem | Main Effect |
|----------|-----------------|-------------|

| Parkinson's Disease | Loss of dopamine neurons | Movement disorders |
| Multiple Sclerosis | Myelin damage | Slowed nerve conduction |
| Stroke | Loss of blood supply | Neuron death |
| Epilepsy | Abnormal neuronal activity | Seizures |
| Peripheral Neuropathy | Damage to peripheral neurons | Pain, numbness, weakness |

Neurons are the biological language of the nervous system.

Complex minds emerge not from a single neuron, but from billions of neurons communicating as one living network.
