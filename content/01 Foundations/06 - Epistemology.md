# Epistemology

> *The study of knowledge and how humans determine what can be known with confidence.*

---

# What is Epistemology?

Epistemology is the branch of philosophy that studies **knowledge**.

It examines:

- What knowledge is.
- How knowledge is acquired.
- How beliefs are justified.
- What makes knowledge reliable.
- What the limits of human knowledge are.

Unlike the [[Scientific Method]], which investigates the natural world, Epistemology investigates **knowledge itself**—asking how humans know whether something is true or merely believed.

---

# Why does Epistemology exist?

Humans constantly claim to know things.

Examples include:

- The Earth orbits the Sun.
- Smoking increases the risk of disease.
- Water boils at 100°C under standard atmospheric pressure.
- This news article is true.
- This memory is accurate.

But how do we know these claims are actually reliable?

Epistemology exists because not every belief is knowledge.

It provides the framework for distinguishing:

- Knowledge from opinion.
- Evidence from assumption.
- Justified belief from unsupported belief.
- Truth from misinformation.

Without epistemology, humans would have no reliable way to evaluate the certainty and reliability of what they believe.

---

# How does Epistemology work?

Epistemology evaluates knowledge by examining three fundamental questions.

It studies:

- Sources of knowledge
- Reliability of evidence
- Justification
- Truth
- Belief
- Certainty
- Skepticism
- Limits of human understanding

Rather than producing new scientific discoveries, epistemology evaluates **how those discoveries become accepted as reliable knowledge.**

# Why should humans care?

Every important decision depends upon knowledge.

Humans constantly ask:

- Can this doctor be trusted?
- Is this investment safe?
- Is this information accurate?
- Is this scientific claim supported by evidence?
- Should I change my opinion?

Epistemology provides the principles for evaluating these questions.

> [!success] Understanding epistemology helps humans
> - Distinguish knowledge from opinion.
> - Evaluate evidence objectively.
> - Recognize uncertainty.
> - Avoid misinformation.
> - Build justified beliefs.
> - Improve lifelong learning.
> - Make more reliable decisions.

# Role in HumanOS

Within HumanOS, **Epistemology** explains how humans determine whether knowledge is reliable.

After learning **how to reason** ([[Logic]]), **how to investigate reality** ([[Scientific Method]]), **how systems interact** ([[Systems Thinking]]), and **how to evaluate claims** ([[Critical Thinking]]), epistemology asks a deeper question:

**What makes knowledge trustworthy?**

It provides the philosophical foundation that prepares the reader for [[Philosophy of Knowledge]], where the value and purpose of knowledge itself are explored.
